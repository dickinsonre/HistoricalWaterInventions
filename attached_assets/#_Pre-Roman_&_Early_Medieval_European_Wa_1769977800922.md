### Invention 1: Tartessian Hydraulic Mining

```markdown
### Tartessian Hydraulic Mining Systems
**Rarity**: Epic | **Date**: 1000 BCE - 500 BCE

> Advanced water-powered mining techniques predating Roman "ruina montium"

**Category**: Mining Hydrology | **Scale**: Industrial

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Water Collection**
   - Dammed mountain streams
   - Large reservoirs (estanques) above mines
   - Channel networks to mining sites
   - Seasonal water storage

2. **Hydraulic Extraction**
   - Water released to strip over in the data.icing to separate ore from soil
   - Channels with riffles to catch heavy metals
   - Settling ponds for gold recovery

3. **Underground Drainage**
   - Adits (horizontal tunnels) for water removal
   - Archimedes screws (possibly pre-Roman origin)
   - Bucket chains (noria precursors)
   - Drainage galleries

4. **Ore Processing**
   - Water-powered crushing (early stamps)
   - Washing tables for separation
   - Amalgamation channels
   - Slag management

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Primary Metals | Gold, silver, copper, tin |
| Major Sites | Río Tinto, Cartagena, Aljustrel |
| Reservoir Capacity | Up to 50,000+ m³ |
| Channel Length | Kilometers from source |
| Era of Peak Operation | 800-500 BCE |
| Metals Exported To | Phoenicia, Greece, Egypt |

**Archaeological Evidence**:
| Site | Findings |
|------|----------|
| Río Tinto | Pre-Roman slag heaps, water channels |
| Aljustrel (Portugal) | Bronze Age mining galleries |
| Cartagena | Tartessian-era workings |
| Huelva | Phoenician-Tartessian harbor works |

**Why It Mattered**:
• Made Tartessos legendarily wealthy (Biblical "Tarshish"?)
• Attracted Phoenician colonization
• Developed techniques Romans later expanded
• Created first industrial-scale mining in Western Europe
• Established Iberia as "El Dorado" of ancient world

**Tartessian Wealth**:
- Greek accounts describe fabulous riches
- Silver so common it made ship anchors
- Possible source of King Solomon's gold
- Traded with Phoenicians from 1100 BCE
- Mysterious collapse ~500 BCE

**Legacy Today**:
→ Río Tinto still mined (5,000 years continuous)
→ Techniques influenced Roman mining
→ Archaeological UNESCO consideration
→ Mining heritage tourism
→ Environmental remediation ongoing

**Robert's Note**:
"When Romans arrived in Iberia, they didn't invent hydraulic 
mining — they industrialized what locals had done for centuries. 
Tartessian engineers had already figured out that water + gravity 
= efficient ore extraction. Rome just scaled it up."
```

### Invention 2: Iberian Cistern Systems

```markdown
### Iberian Urban Cistern Networks
**Rarity**: Rare | **Date**: 600 BCE - 19 BCE

> Rock-cut cisterns supplying hilltop fortified settlements (oppida)

**Category**: Water Storage | **Scale**: Urban

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Cistern Construction**
   - Cut into bedrock beneath settlements
   - Bottle-shaped for structural stability
   - Plastered with hydraulic lime
   - Capacity: 50-500+ m³ per cistern

2. **Collection System**
   - Rooftop collection from buildings
   - Paved courtyards as catchments
   - Street drainage channels
   - Settling basins before cisterns

3. **Water Treatment**
   - Settling chambers remove sediment
   - Charcoal filters in some sites
   - Ventilation shafts maintain freshness
   - Regular cleaning protocols

4. **Distribution**
   - Draw wells at cistern tops
   - Some gravity-fed to fountains
   - Rationing systems during siege
   - Dedicated cisterns for different uses

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Cistern Depth | 5-15 meters |
| Typical Capacity | 100-300 m³ |
| Largest Known | 500+ m³ (Ullastret) |
| Construction | Rock-cut with plaster lining |
| Settlement Type | Hilltop oppida |
| Major Sites | Ullastret, Sagunto, Numancia |

**Notable Sites**:
| Site | Features |
|------|----------|
| Ullastret | Multiple large cisterns, Greek influence |
| Sagunto | Survived 8-month Roman siege |
| Numancia | Famous last stand against Rome |
| Azaila | Complex underground system |

**Why It Mattered**:
• Enabled hilltop defensive settlements
• Water security during sieges (critical)
• Supported populations of 1,000-10,000
• Sophisticated urban planning
• Technical knowledge predating Roman influence

**Siege Warfare Significance**:
- Sagunto: 8 months against Hannibal (219 BCE)
- Numancia: 13 years resistance to Rome
- Water supply = defensive capability
- Romans often had to wait for cisterns to empty

**Legacy Today**:
→ Many cisterns still visible at archaeological sites
→ Influenced Roman provincial engineering
→ Model for medieval castle cisterns
→ Tourism at Iberian archaeological parks
→ Studies informing arid climate water storage

**Robert's Note**:
"The Iberians built their towns on hilltops for defense, 
which created a water problem — no rivers up there. Their 
solution was engineering their way out: massive underground 
cisterns that could sustain a city through drought and siege 
alike. Numancia held out for 13 years."
```
