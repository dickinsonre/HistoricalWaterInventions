### Invention 1: Longhouse River Settlements

```markdown
### Longhouse River Settlement System
**Rarity**: Epic | **Date**: 2000 BCE - Present

> Elevated communal dwellings engineered for flood survival and river transportation

**Category**: Flood Architecture | **Still Active**: Yes

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Site Selection**
   - Built on riverbanks (primary transportation)
   - Elevated terraces above flood line
   - Near tributary confluences (trade access)
   - Forest buffer maintained upstream (flood control)

2. **Structural Engineering**
   - Ironwood (belian) posts: 3-6 meters tall
   - Entire structure raised on stilts
   - Single building houses 20-100 families
   - Length: 100-400+ meters
   - Withstands 5-10 meter flood surges

3. **Water Access Integration**
   - Bathing platforms at river level
   - Fish traps beneath longhouse
   - Boat moorings integrated
   - Rainwater collection from massive roof
   - Gravity-fed bamboo aqueducts from springs

4. **Flood Response Design**
   - Removable lower platforms
   - Storage elevated in roof space
   - Boats stored beneath structure
   - Emergency evacuation routes
   - Community alarm systems

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Longest Historical | 400+ meters (Iban) |
| Height Above Ground | 3-6 meters |
| Families Per Longhouse | 20-100 |
| Primary Wood | Belian (ironwood) - lasts 100+ years |
| Roof Material | Ironwood shingles or palm thatch |
| Flood Tolerance | 5-10 meter water rise |

**Longhouse Types by People**:
| People | Region | Distinctive Feature |
|--------|--------|---------------------|
| Iban | Sarawak | Longest structures, warrior tradition |
| Bidayuh | Sarawak | Hilltop locations, head houses |
| Kayan/Kenyah | Central Borneo | Elaborate carvings, stratified society |
| Kadazan-Dusun | Sabah | Valley locations, rice focus |
| Punan/Penan | Interior | Semi-mobile, forest-adapted |

**Why It Mattered**:
• Community survival during extreme floods
• Social cohesion (entire village under one roof)
• Defense against raids (single entry point)
• Efficient land use (vertical living)
• River transportation hub
• Cultural identity preservation
• Still inhabited today (adapted versions)

**Modern Adaptations**:
- Concrete/steel stilts in some areas
- Modern roofing materials
- Electricity and plumbing integrated
- Tourism adaptations
- Government resettlement challenges

**Legacy Today**:
→ UNESCO consideration for heritage status
→ Tourism destination (Sarawak Cultural Village)
→ Model for flood-resilient tropical housing
→ Climate adaptation lessons
→ Still primary housing for some communities

**Robert's Note**:

social cohesion, and transportation access. A single structure
```

### Invention 2: Peat Swamp Canal Systems

```markdown
### Peat Swamp Canal Systems (Tatah/Anjir)
**Rarity**: Rare | **Date**: 500 CE - Present

> Hand-dug canals through peat swamps enabling transportation and agriculture

**Category**: Canal Engineering | **Still Active**: Yes (modified)

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Canal Excavation**
   - Hand-dug through peat (3-15 meters deep)
   - Width: 3-10 meters
   - Length: kilometers to tens of kilometers
   - Spoil piled on banks (creates elevated land)

2. **Water Level Management**
   - Wooden sluice gates (tabat) control flow
   - Seasonal adjustment for planting
   - Maintains peat moisture (prevents fire)
   - Connects to river systems

3. **Multi-Purpose Function**
   - Transportation (boats primary travel)
   - Agriculture (raised banks for crops)
   - Fisheries (canal fishing)
   - Water supply (filtered through peat)
   - Fire prevention (wet peat doesn't burn)

4. **Peat Hydrology Understanding**
   - Peat acts as giant sponge
   - Must stay saturated or oxidizes/burns
   - Canals too deep drain peat (destructive)
   - Traditional depth maintains water table

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Peat Depth | 3-15 meters |
| Canal Width | 3-10 meters |
| Traditional Canal Depth | 1-2 meters (shallow by design) |
| Network Extent | Thousands of kilometers (historical) |
| Construction Rate | ~50-100 meters/day (hand dug) |
| Regions | South/Central Kalimantan, Sarawak coast |

**Traditional vs. Modern Canals**:
| Aspect | Traditional | Modern (Colonial/Industrial) |
|--------|-------------|------------------------------|
| Depth | 1-2m (shallow) | 5-10m (deep) |
| Purpose | Transport + fishing | Drainage for plantations |
| Peat Impact | Maintained saturation | Drains and destroys peat |
| Fire Risk | Low | High (drained peat burns) |
| Carbon Impact | Neutral | Massive CO2 release |

**Why It Mattered**:
• Opened inaccessible swamp regions
• Created agricultural land on banks
• Sustainable peat management
• Transportation network
• Fishery development
• Fire prevention through saturation

**Modern Crisis**:
- Colonial/industrial deep canals drain peat
- Drained peat = catastrophic fires (1997, 2015, 2019)
- Carbon release = global climate impact
- Traditional knowledge being revisited
- "Rewetting" projects restore water levels

**Legacy Today**:
→ Traditional methods studied for peat restoration
→ "Canal blocking" mimics traditional tabat system
→ Indigenous knowledge recognized by scientists
→ Peatland restoration projects
→ Climate mitigation through rewetting

**Robert's Note**:
"Indigenous Borneans understood something colonial engineers didn't: 
peat swamps need to stay wet. Traditional shallow canals transported 
goods while keeping peat saturated. Deep drainage canals caused 
fires visible from space. We're now paying billions to restore what 
traditional systems maintained for free."

**Diagram Description**:
[Cross-section through peat swamp: surface vegetation → water table 
→ peat layer (3-15m) → mineral soil. Traditional shallow canal 
shown maintaining water table vs. modern deep canal draining peat. 
Arrows showing: saturated peat = stable, drained peat = subsidence/fire]
```

```markdown
**Modern Recognition**:

**Legacy Today**:

**Robert's Note**:
```

### Invention 4: Bamboo Aqueduct Systems (Talang)

```markdown
### Bamboo Aqueduct Systems (Talang)
**Rarity**: Rare | **Date**: Ancient - Present

> Gravity-fed water supply using split bamboo channels across kilometers

**Category**: Water Supply | **Still Active**: Rural areas

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Source Selection**
   - Mountain springs or clean streams
   - Above village elevation
   - Forested catchment protected
   - Year-round flow essential

2. **Bamboo Channel Construction**
   - Large bamboo (10-15cm diameter) split lengthwise
   - Internal nodes removed
   - Sections overlapped and tied
   - Supported on forked sticks or trees
   - Follows contour with gentle slope

3. **Gradient Engineering**
   - Slope: approximately 1-2% (1-2m per 100m)
   - Too steep = erosion of bamboo
   - Too flat = stagnant water
   - Adjusted by support height
   - Crosses valleys on high supports

4. **Distribution System**
   - Main channel to village center
   - Smaller bamboo splits to houses
   - Bathing areas at termini
   - Irrigation offtakes for gardens
   - Overflow to drainage

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Bamboo Species | Gigantochloa, Dendrocalamus |
| Channel Diameter | 10-15 cm |
| Typical Length | 500m - 5 km |
| Flow Rate | 5-20 liters/minute |
| Lifespan | 2-5 years (replaced regularly) |
| Gradient | 1-2% optimal |

**Construction Process**:
| Step | Activity | Time |
|------|----------|------|
| 1 | Harvest mature bamboo | 1-2 days |
| 2 | Split and clean channels | 1 day |
| 3 | Survey route from source | 1 day |
| 4 | Install supports | 2-3 days |
| 5 | Lay and connect channels | 2-5 days |
| 6 | Test and adjust | 1 day |
| **Total** | **Community effort** | **1-2 weeks** |

**Why It Mattered**:
• Clean water without pumps
• Zero energy cost
• Local materials only
• Community construction
• Easily repaired/replaced
• Forest protection incentive

**Advantages Over Alternatives**:
| Method | Cost | Energy | Maintenance | Water Quality |

**Legacy Today**:

→ Eco-lodge tourist attraction
→ Model for low-tech water supply
→ Permaculture movement adopted
→ Climate-resilient technology

**Robert's Note**:
```

### Invention 5: Floating Rice Cultivation

```markdown
### Floating Rice Cultivation (Padi Pasang Surut)
**Rarity**: Rare | **Date**: Ancient - Present

> Rice varieties and techniques adapted to tidal flood zones

**Category**: Tidal Agriculture | **Still Active**: Yes

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Tidal Zone Selection**
   - Coastal/riverine areas with tidal influence
   - Fresh or brackish water (low salinity)
   - Daily or seasonal flooding cycles
   - Sediment deposition zones (fertile)

2. **Adapted Rice Varieties**
   - Stems elongate as water rises (up to 5m!)
   - Rapid growth during flood (10+ cm/day possible)

   - Deep root system anchors in mud

3. **Minimal Field Preparation**
   - No irrigation infrastructure needed
   - No bunding/levees in traditional system
   - Broadcast seeding or transplanting
   - Tidal flooding provides water
   - Sediment provides nutrients

4. **Harvest Adaptation**
   - Harvest by boat in flooded fields
   - Or wait for dry season drainage
   - Stalks cut above water level
   - Threshing on floating platforms

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|

| Daily Growth Rate | 5-10 cm (during flood) |

| Yield | Lower than irrigated (1-2 tons/ha) |
```

### Invention 6: Rattan Fish Traps (Bubu)

```markdown
### Rattan Fish Traps (Bubu)
**Rarity**: Common | **Date**: Ancient - Present

> Ingenious basket traps using forest materials for river and coastal fishing

**Category**: Fisheries | **Still Active**: Yes

1. **Trap Construction**
   - Woven from rattan or bamboo strips
   - Cone or cylinder shape
   - Funnel entrance (easy in, hard out)
   - Size: 30cm to 2+ meters
   - Takes 1-3 days to weave

2. **Funnel Mechanism**
   - Fish swim into wide opening
   - Funnel narrows inward
   - Fish cannot find exit
   - Multiple funnels increase catch
   - Non-destructive to fish

3. **Deployment Methods**
   - **River**: Anchored facing upstream
   - **Tidal**: Set in channels during ebb
   - **Coastal**: Weighted on seafloor
   - **Baited**: Fruit, fish scraps, rice bran
   - **Unbaited**: Natural movement

4. **Selective Fishing**
   - Gap size selects fish size
   - Small fish escape (sustainability)
   - Specific designs for target species
   - Live capture (can release unwanted)

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Materials | Rattan, bamboo, palm fiber |
| Size Range | 30cm - 2+ meters |
| Construction Time | 4-24 hours |
| Lifespan | 6 months - 2 years |

| Depth Range | Surface to 30+ meters |

**Advantages**:
```

### Invention 1: Kuk Swamp Drainage Agriculture

```markdown
### Kuk Swamp Drainage Agriculture
**Rarity**: Legendary | **Date**: 7000 BCE - Present

> The world's oldest known wetland agriculture—drainage channels predating 
> Egyptian civilization by 4,000 years

**Category**: Wetland Agriculture | **UNESCO**: World Heritage Site (2008)

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Swamp Selection**
   - Highland valley swamps (1,500-1,700m elevation)
   - Year-round water supply
   - Rich organic soils
   - Frost-free swamp floor (water thermal mass)

2. **Drainage Channel System**
   - Network of ditches dug by hand (digging sticks)
   - Main channels: 2-4 meters wide, 1-2 meters deep
   - Smaller channels subdivide plots
   - Water table lowered to create dry planting surface
   - Spoil (dug soil) piled into raised beds

3. **Planting Surfaces**
   - Raised beds between channels
   - Mounded soil for root crops
   - Taro primary crop (7000 BCE onwards)
   - Bananas, sugarcane, yams added later
   - Sweet potato (400 years ago) revolutionized

4. **Water Level Management**
   - Channels drained or blocked seasonally
   - Maintained moist but not flooded conditions
   - Prevented crop rot
   - Controlled with wooden barriers

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Site Elevation | 1,560 meters |
| Oldest Channels | ~9,000 years old (7000 BCE) |
| Total Drained Area | 116 hectares (Kuk site) |
| Channel Depth | 0.5-2 meters |
| Phase 1 (Oldest) | Simple basin drainage |
| Phase 6 (Recent) | Grid system with raised beds |

**Archaeological Phases at Kuk**:
| Phase | Date | Technology |
|-------|------|------------|

| 3 | 2000-1000 BCE | Major ditch networks |
| 4 | 100 BCE - 100 CE | Grid layouts |
| 5 | 300-700 CE | Intensive drainage |
| 6 | 400 years ago - present | Sweet potato integration |

**Why It Mattered**:
• **Oldest wetland agriculture in the world**
• Independent invention (no external influence)
• Enabled permanent highland settlement
• Supported dense populations
• Frost protection (swamp warmer than slopes)
• Led to taro/banana domestication
• Continuous use for 9,000 years

**Global Significance**:
- One of only 3-4 independent agricultural origins
- Predates Mesopotamian irrigation by millennia
- Evidence of complex engineering before metal tools
- Challenges "cradle of civilization" narratives
- UNESCO World Heritage Site 2008

**Legacy Today**:
→ Traditional gardens still cultivated nearby
→ Archaeological tourism
→ Global recognition of PNG innovation
→ Sweet potato remains staple crop
→ Model for wetland agriculture
```

### Invention 3: Sago Palm Water Processing

```markdown
### Sago Palm Water Processing
**Rarity**: Rare | **Date**: Ancient - Present

> Water-intensive extraction of starch from swamp palms—feeding millions

**Category**: Food Processing | **Still Active**: Yes (staple food)

---

**How It Works**:

1. **Palm Selection and Felling**
   - Metroxylon sagu palm (sago palm)
   - Grows in freshwater swamps
   - Harvested at 10-15 years (before flowering)
   - Single tree = 200-800 kg of starch
   - Felling by axe (traditionally stone)

2. **Pith Extraction**
   - Trunk split lengthwise
   - Soft pith chopped/rasped out
   - Traditional: stone or wooden rasps
   - Modern: metal-toothed tools
   - Pith collected in containers

3. **Water Washing (Critical Step)**
   - Pith placed in washing trough
   - Water poured through repeatedly
   - Starch washes out with water
   - Fiber remains in trough (discarded)
   - Starchy water collected below

4. **Sedimentation**
   - Starchy water settles in containers
   - Starch sinks to bottom (dense)
   - Water decanted off top
   - Fresh water added, mixed, settled (repeated)
   - Purified starch collected as wet mass

5. **Storage/Use**
   - Wet starch stored in water (weeks)
   - Dried for long-term storage
   - Cooked as porridge, pancakes, dumplings
   - Roasted into dry "sago pearls"

**Technical Specifications**:
| Spec | Value |
|------|-------|
| Starch Yield/Palm | 200-800 kg |
| Processing Time | 2-5 days per palm |
| Water Required | 500-2,000 liters per palm |
| Storage (wet) | 2-4 weeks |
| Storage (dried) | Months to years |
| Calories/Palm | 800,000-3,200,000 kcal |

**Sago Processing Steps**:
| Step | Water Use | Time |
|------|-----------|------|
| Felling | None | 1-2 hours |
```
